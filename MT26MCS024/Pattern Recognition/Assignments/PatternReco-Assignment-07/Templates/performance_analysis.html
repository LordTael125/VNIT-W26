Core Theory

Mathematical & Algorithmic Analysis (Pattern Recognition Lab)

1. Role of Feature Standardization

The K-Nearest Neighbors classifier relies on Euclidean distance in D-dimensional feature space:

\[ d(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}_i) = \sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^D (x_j - x_{i,j})^2} \]

If one feature has a variance of \(10^5\) (e.g. Income) and another has a variance of \(10^0\) (e.g. Age), the unscaled distance is almost entirely dominated by the large-magnitude feature. Applying Scikit-Learn StandardScaler normalizes each feature to \(\mu = 0\) and \(\sigma = 1\), giving every feature an equitable contribution to proximity calculations.

2. The Elbow Method & Bias-Variance Tradeoff

The choice of hyperparameter \(K\) governs the model's complexity:

  • Small K (e.g., K = 1): High variance, zero training bias. Sensitive to outliers and noise in individual samples.
  • Large K: High bias, low variance. Over-smoothes the decision boundary, blurring class boundaries.

The Elbow Method identifies the sharp bend in the 'K' vs Error Rate curve where the rate of error reduction decelerates, capturing maximum predictive capacity with minimal model complexity.

3. KNN vs. Bayes Classifier (Non-Parametric vs. Generative Parametric)

K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)

Non-parametric & Instance-based: Makes no assumptions about underlying probability distributions. Approximates posterior probabilities purely from the local neighborhood vote density. Highly flexible with non-linear decision boundaries, but computation scales with training dataset size.

Bayes Classifier (Gaussian Naive Bayes)

Parametric & Generative: Models class-conditional densities \(P(x \mid C_k)\) using normal distributions with mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\). Inference is instantaneous: \(\mathcal{O}(D)\) time per query instance. Highly robust with independent features, but subject to bias if feature normality or independence is violated.
