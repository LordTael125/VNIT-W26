This course is not about making you better programmers.
It is about changing the way you think.
Programming solves technical problems.
Systems Engineering solves systems problems.
Dialectic thinking solves problems where even the de nition of the problem is disputed.

Technical thinking asks: How can I solve it?
Systems thinking asks: Why does the whole system behave this way?
Dialectic thinking asks: What assumptions are competing, and how can they be synthesized into a
better solution?

Why Study Systems Engineering in the AI Era?
Traditional engineer asked
How do I build it?
AI increasingly answers that.
Systems Engineer asks
•Should we build it?
•For whom?
•Under what constraints?
•What risks will emerge?
•What happens after deployment?
•What are the unintended consequences?
AI cannot answer these independently because they involve values, tradeoffs, governance, and human judgment.

The age of AI does not reduce the importance of Systems Engineering—
it increases it.
As machines become better at solving technical problems, engineers must
become better at asking the right questions, understanding complex systems,
and making responsible decisions.

If AI is becoming good at technical problem solving, then what kind of
thinking must humans develop?

That is why we study Systems Engineering.
