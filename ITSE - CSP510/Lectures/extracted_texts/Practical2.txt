1. Why this Practical?
In Practical 1, you learned to recognize a
system and identify its purpose, stakeholders,
components, interactions, boundary,
environment, and emergent behaviour.
However, a system cannot be understood only
from its components or from a single
observation. Its condition changes with time,
events, operating context, stakeholder actions,
and environmental in uences. The same
apparent system state may also have different
operational signi cance in different situations.
In this practical, you will examine one system
dynamically—how it changes, behaves,
performs its functions, and responds to events.

2. Learning Objectives
After completing this practical, you should be
able to:
•
distinguish among the state, situation,
behaviour, process, and function of a
system;
•
identify variables that describe the state
of a system;
•
recognize how context and
relationships transform a state into a
situation;
•
trace changes in the system following
signi cant events;
•
distinguish what a system does from
how it does it;
•
identify intended, unintended, and
emergent behaviour; and
•
analyze how a change in one part of a
system affects its overall functioning.

C. Construct Situations from States
A state describes the condition of the system at
an instant. A situation includes that state
together with the relevant context,
relationships, history, stakeholder concerns,
and likely consequences.
Construct two situations having substantially
the same observable state but different
operational signi cance.
For each situation, identify:
•
the common observable state;
•
the surrounding context;
•
relevant stakeholder relationships;
•
preceding events or history;
•
stakeholder concerns;
•
possible consequences; and
•
why the required response may be
different.
The differences must arise from context or
relationships—not merely by changing the
values of the state variables.

F. Distinguish Function from Process
Identify three important functions performed
by the system.

what outcome the function is intended

•

Then answer:
1. Can the same function be achieved

2. Can the process be completed while the
intended function remains unful lled?

G. Introduce a Change in the System
Propose one realistic change in a component,
rule, resource, stakeholder action, technology,
or environmental condition.
Trace its effects on:
•
system state;
•
stakeholder relationships;
•
process execution;
•
system behaviour;
•
intended function; and
•
possible unintended consequences.
Conclude whether the change improves the
system as a whole or only one part of it.
