What is Systems Engineering?
Systems Engineering is about understanding and designing complex, interconnected 
systems — not just isolated code. It equips you to:
•See the bigger picture of how your software ﬁts into real-world operations.
•Understand how decisions, feedback, delays, and human behavior shape outcomes.
•Solve real problems in healthcare, transport, agriculture, public systems, cyber-
physical environments, and more.

Let us begin with a Story…
Story: "The Programmer and the Pump"
Once upon a time in a small town in India, there 
was a young computer science graduate named 
Aditya. Fresh out of college, Aditya landed his 
ﬁrst job with the municipal smart water 
management project.
His task seemed simple:
“Write code to automate the operation of water 
pumps based on demand and tank levels.”
Aditys was excited. He built a clean, efﬁcient algorithm. 
It read the water level, calculated demand, and turned pumps 
on and off. 
It worked beautifully in simulation.
But when deployed, chaos followed.
•Pumps turned on at night, wasting electricity.
•Water overﬂowed or didn't reach certain homes.
•The villagers blamed the municipality.
•The municipality blamed the “buggy code.”
Aditys was stunned. “The code works. I followed the logic. 
Why did it fail?”


--- PAGE 5 ---
The Systems Engineer Arrives
Enter Angel, a Systems Engineer with a background in 
both software and public systems.
She asked questions Aditya hadn’t thought of:
•"Who uses the water and when?"
•"What time does electricity cost less?"
•"What’s the role of the operator in switching pumps 
manually?"
•"What if there’s a pipe leak? What’s the feedback 
loop?"
They went into the ﬁeld together. Aditya saw:
•Some tanks were not level-calibrated properly.
•Operators bypassed automation due to distrust.
•Power cuts interfered with the timing.
•People ﬁlled water as a precaution even when they 
didn’t need it — triggering false high demand.
The Realization
Aditya ﬁnally realized:
“My code was correct, but my system was blind.”
He wasn’t just automating pumps — 
He was intervening in a living, human-technical 
system.
To succeed, he needed to:
•Map the entire system (Concept)
•Design with constraints and human behavior in 
mind (Strategy)
•Simulate and adjust for real-world operation 
(Operation)
The Mindset Change
From a coder who just wrote instructions, 
Aditya became a System Thinker who:
•Observed ﬁrst,
•Modeled behavior,
•Designed for robustness,
•And collaborated with people — not just code.
Moral
“In Systems Engineering, success lies not in perfect code, but in 
understanding imperfect systems.”
Code is not the solution — it is just one part of a system that must be 
understood, inﬂuenced, and designed systemically.

Dialectic Problem Solving
Suppose a government says
"Install facial recognition everywhere."
Engineer says
"It works."
Privacy activist says
"It violates rights."
Police say
"It increases security.”
Citizen says
"I don't trust surveillance."
All are technically correct.
Now the problem cannot be solved by programming.
It requires
•balancing objectives
•resolving contradictions
•creating a better design.
That is dialectic thinking.
Not
Who is right?
But
How do conﬂicting truths produce a better system?
So, dialectical thinking can be understood as
The ability to understand reality by identifying contradictions, examining opposing 
viewpoints, and synthesizing a better understanding instead of accepting a single 
perspective.
Example 1: Flyover 
Construction
Technical Thinking
There is trafﬁc congestion. Build 
a ﬂyover.
Dialectical Thinking
•Will it reduce congestion?
•Will it induce more trafﬁc?
•Will pedestrians suffer?
•What about public transport?
•Can trafﬁc management solve 
the problem instead?
Conclusion
The problem is not simply "lack 
of road," but the interaction 
between roads, vehicles, public 
transport, and human behavior.

Engineering

This course is not about making you better programmers.
It is about changing the way you think.
Programming solves technical problems.
Systems Engineering solves systems problems.
Dialectic thinking solves problems where even the deﬁnition of the problem is disputed.
Technical thinking asks: How can I solve it?
Systems thinking asks: Why does the whole system behave this way?
Dialectic thinking asks: What assumptions are competing, and how can they be synthesized into a 
better solution?
Systems Engineering is just not another branch of engineering. It is 
the disciplined application of systems thinking and dialectic thinking 
to design, build, operate, and improve complex socio-technical 
systems.
Engineering in the Age of AI
AI is changing engineering—but not 
replacing engineers.
AI is becoming very good at
•Writing code
•Designing algorithms
•Optimizing parameters
•Generating reports
•Detecting patterns
•Assisting design
AI still struggles with
•Understanding the real human problem
•Balancing conﬂicting stakeholder needs
•Deﬁning system boundaries
•Ethical decision making
•Responsibility and accountability
•Long-term societal impact
Therefore
AI increasingly solves technical 
tasks.
Engineers must increasingly solve 
systems problems.

Why Study Systems Engineering in the AI Era?
Traditional engineer asked
How do I build it?
AI increasingly answers that.
Systems Engineer asks
•Should we build it?
•For whom?
•Under what constraints?
•What risks will emerge?
•What happens after deployment?
•What are the unintended consequences?
AI cannot answer these independently because they involve values, trade-
offs, governance, and human judgment.
The age of AI does not reduce the importance of Systems Engineering—
it increases it.
As machines become better at solving technical problems, engineers must 
become better at asking the right questions, understanding complex systems, 
and making responsible decisions.
If AI is becoming good at technical problem solving, then what kind of 
thinking must humans develop?
Technical Thinking 
        ↓ 
Systems Thinking 
        ↓ 
Dialectic Thinking 
        ↓ 
Systems Engineering
The Industrial Age rewarded those who could do the work.
The Information Age rewarded those who could automate the work.
The AI Age will reward those who can deﬁne the right problems, design 
responsible systems, and integrate humans, technology, and society.
That is why we study Systems Engineering.
