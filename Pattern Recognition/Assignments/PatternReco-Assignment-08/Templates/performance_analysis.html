Theoretical Foundations Mathematical Principles: Ho-Kashyap Procedure vs. KNN & Bayes

1. The Ho-Kashyap Procedure & Separability Guarantee

Unlike standard Perceptron or Fixed-Margin relaxation algorithms that may loop indefinitely on non-separable data, Ho-Kashyap simultaneously optimizes both the weight vector \(\mathbf{a}\) and positive margin vector \(\mathbf{b}\):

\[ J_H(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) = \frac{1}{2} \|Y\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}\|^2 \quad \text{subject to } \mathbf{b} > \mathbf{0} \]
  • Weight update: \(\mathbf{a}(k) = Y^\dagger \mathbf{b}(k)\) where \(Y^\dagger = (Y^T Y)^{-1}Y^T\) is the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse.
  • Margin update: \(\mathbf{b}(k+1) = \mathbf{b}(k) + 2\eta \mathbf{e}^+(k)\) where \(\mathbf{e}^+(k) = \max(\mathbf{0}, Y\mathbf{a}(k) - \mathbf{b}(k))\).
  • Convergence Proof: If classes are linearly separable, the squared error \(\|\mathbf{e}(k)\|^2 \to 0\) in finite iterations. If linearly non-separable, \(\mathbf{e}(k) \le \mathbf{0}\) with \(\mathbf{e}(k) \ne \mathbf{0}\) occurs, providing a mathematical proof of non-separability.

2. Impact of Learning Rate \(\eta\) & Standardization

The learning rate \(\eta \in [0.1, 0.9]\) governs the step size for adjusting the margin vector \(\mathbf{b}\):

\[ \Delta \mathbf{b}(k) = 2\eta \mathbf{e}^+(k) \]
  • Small \(\eta\) (\(0.1 - 0.2\)): Smooth, conservative margin adjustments; requires more iterations to reach convergence.
  • Larger \(\eta\) (\(0.6 - 0.9\)): Faster convergence in fewer iterations; optimal when the error rate remains stable.
  • Role of StandardScaler: Feature standardization prevents large-scale continuous features from dominating the pseudo-inverse \(Y^\dagger\), balancing feature contributions and improving linear generalization.

3. Comparative Paradigms: Linear Discriminant vs. Instance-Based vs. Generative

Ho-Kashyap Linear Classifier

Linear Discriminant: Directly optimizes a hyperparameter-free hyperplane \(g(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{a}^T \mathbf{x}_{\text{aug}}\). Extremely fast inference (\(\mathcal{O}(d)\)). Tests linear separability rigorously, but constrained to planar decision boundaries.

K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)

Non-Parametric: Makes zero distributional assumptions. Capable of learning arbitrary non-linear boundaries. Hyperparameter \(K\) tuned via Elbow Method, but test-time evaluation requires pairwise distance computations against all training instances (\(\mathcal{O}(Nd)\)).

Gaussian Naive Bayes

Parametric Generative: Models \(P(\mathbf{x}|C_k)\) and priors \(P(C_k)\). Highly resilient to small sample sizes and scales efficiently (\(\mathcal{O}(d)\)), but assumes conditional feature independence given the class.
